None of these images was photographed. Each began as grey noise and was adjusted, seven hundred and sixty-eight times, until any thirty-two-pixel glimpse of it left the model certain of a single word. There are four for each word, from four different beginnings.

The model is a vision transformer trained on fifty thousand tiny photographs from CIFAR-10: airplanes, automobiles, birds, cats, deer, dogs, frogs, horses, ships and trucks. It knows nothing else. These are what those ten words mean to it.

Every pixel is a point, set back or brought forward by when the model decided it: what settled first sits furthest away, and the last details to resolve come nearest. Choose a dream and it develops again, each point flying in out of the fog at the moment its pixel settled.

The images are parameterized in a decorrelated Fourier basis and optimized through random crops, flips and rescalings, after Olah, Mordvintsev & Schubert, Feature Visualization (2017), and Mordvintsev's DeepDream (2015). Each image's contrast is stretched once; nothing else is retouched.
